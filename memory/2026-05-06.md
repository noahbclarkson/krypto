### The Sharpe Taxonomy Problem

We have two completely different Sharpe definitions and we use them interchangeably:

1. **Daily account Sharpe** (from `examples/live_bot_exact_equity.rs`): ~1.01 — the real number, computed from compounded daily returns
2. **Per-window walk-forward Sharpe** (mean of per-window Sharpe ratios): ~5.5 — inflated ~5x, not comparable

The per-window average Sharpe inflates because: mean of (mean/σ × √252) across windows ≠ (total_mean / total_std) × √252. The correct daily equity Sharpe is ~1.01. We should only ever cite 1.01 on Discord charts.

### Are We Curve-Fitting?

**Yes, in a subtle way.** The 9-universe grid has been used consistently since April. We're optimizing Turtle parameters (EP, ATR period/mult, CHAND P/M, VOL_LOOKBACK, etc.) against the same 9-universe / 6-window grid repeatedly. The grid itself hasn't been validated as representative — we've just learned it through repetition.

The ATR_RANK case is the clearest evidence: we know it's non-stationary (held-out fails), but we keep sweeping it because it produces "winners." T=5.0 "won" — but winning in a non-stationary parameter space means nothing.

### Fee Model Uncertainty

- Maker fill could be 40% in sustained bear markets (degraded execution)

## Biggest Blind Spots

### Blind Spot 1: We only test in a learned universe
The 9-universe grid has been run dozens of times since April. We know which pairs trend well (BTC, ETH, SOL, DOGE) and which don't (LTC, EOS, BCH). We exclude the failing ones from production (Base5). But we have no validation that our parameters generalize beyond the 6 pairs we've implicitly chosen as "crypto." T80 (OOS validation on UNI/MATIC/AVAX) is listed as a priority but not built. This is the most important test we could run.

### Blind Spot 2: Equity is dangerously concentrated
T73 audit: top 10 log contributors = 82.8% of total compounded log return. One bad filter decision can destroy 0.5x of equity. We've documented the guardrail (preserve top winners) but we haven't built automated detection — we only know about this because we manually audited it. If we add a filter in the future without running the top-winner preservation test, we could silently destroy the tail.

### Blind Spot 3: We test all strategies on the same harness system
Every strategy gets tested on the walk-forward harness. But the walk-forward harness has known differences from the exact live bot (VOL_LOOKBACK ranking, different exit timing, different portfolio construction). A strategy that passes the harness might still fail on the exact live path (as T72 and T69 demonstrated). We don't have an integration test that runs candidate strategies through the actual `src/live/bot.rs` logic path.

### Blind Spot 4: The 2021/2022 chop regimes are underweighted
Our per-year decomposition shows 2021 (+35.5%) and 2022 (+10.9%) are the weakest years — exactly the chop/bear chop regimes where Turtle whipsaws. We know this but we keep testing on the full history where 2018, 2020, 2023, 2024, 2025 are all strong bull/bear-crisis years that inflate our pass rates. A strategy that passes the full history might only pass because of the high-SHARPE bull windows. The worst-case scenarios (2021 chop) are underweighted.

### Blind Spot 5: We keep documenting loops instead of closing them
T70 (4 weeks), T61 (6 weeks), T53 (5 weeks). Each session produces a PLAN.md update. The next session reads it, updates it again, and nothing changes. The execute-or-close rule exists but keeps being violated.

## The 3 Most Promising Unbuilt Ideas

### 1. C16: Regime-Conditional Chandelier Multiplier (mechanistically different from the dead vol-contingent attempt)
**Why it's promising:** The prior vol-contingent Chandelier failed because 21-bar realized vol vs 252-bar history barely crosses 0.75/0.25 thresholds — the mechanism was right but the trigger was too slow-moving.

**New hypothesis:** Use *regime* (high/low trend probability from SMA21 vs SMA200 classification) to condition the Chandelier multiplier, not realized vol rank. In high-trend regimes, use CHAND_MULT=2.0 (tighter exit, protect gains); in low-trend regimes, use CHAND_MULT=2.50 (looser exit, let winners run). This is mechanistically different from vol-rank because regime classification is binary and fast-moving (changes ~2-4x per year), whereas vol rank is continuous and slow.

**Test:** CHAND_MULT ∈ {2.0, 2.25, 2.50} conditioned on regime. Walk-forward 9 universes × 6 windows. Must beat both static CHAND_MULT=2.0 and CHAND_MULT=2.30 on pass rate AND Sharpe.

**Risk:** Regime classification might be too slow (SMA200 takes 200 bars to turn). But if it works even partially, it's free improvement.

### 2. C17: Consecutive-Bar Momentum Filter (entry quality without reducing trade count)
**Why it's promising:** Current Turtle entry (`close > max(close, EP)`) fires immediately on the bar that breaks the high. A consecutive-bar version requires 2 consecutive closes above the Turtle entry level before entering. This filters false breakouts that immediately reverse.

**Hypothesis:** In choppy regimes, the first close above the high often fails immediately. Requiring a second consecutive close filters ~20-30% of false breakouts while preserving most genuine trends.

**Test:** Compare Turtle entry (EP, 1-bar confirmation) vs Turtle entry + 1 additional consecutive close above entry level. Walk-forward 9 universes × 6 windows.

**Why it's different from ATR_ENTRY_MULT:** ATR_ENTRY_MULT filters based on volatility-adjusted proximity to entry. Consecutive-bar filter is about *time-confirmation* — requiring the breakout to sustain, not just occur.

### 3. C18: Maker-Fill Rate Stress Test
**Why it's promising:** We validated 70.6% maker fill on FTX crash (one window). We assume it holds. But what if maker fill degrades to 40% in a sustained bear market? We need a stress test that reports equity as a function of maker fill rate (30% to 80% in 5pp steps) so we know our actual risk range.

**Current state:** We have a point estimate (2.81x) at assumed 70% maker fill. We don't know if equity is 2.5x or 3.2x at 50% maker fill.

**Output:** A table: maker_fill_rate → fee-adjusted equity → fee-adjusted Sharpe. So we can say "at 50% maker fill, expect ~2.1x and Sharpe ~0.7 — still viable but tighter margin."

This isn't a new strategy — it's critical risk quantification we should have had before even considering testnet deployment.
