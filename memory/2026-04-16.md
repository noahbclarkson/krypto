### 2. THREE SHARPE NUMBERS, NONE COMPARABLE

| Metric | Value | What it measures |
|--------|-------|-----------------|
| Walk-forward per-window avg | **6.29** | Mean of per-window Sharpe ratios (each window: mean/σ × √252) |
| Daily equity Sharpe (NoDOGE) | **1.04** | Annualized std/mean of actual compounded daily equity returns |
| Progress chart (Chandelier) | **~2.5** | Milestone-aggregated (NOT daily equity) |

**The 6.29 is mathematically incomparable to the others.** Each window's Sharpe is itself a ratio — averaging ratios is a known statistical error. The actual compounded equity curve gives 1.04. This is NOT a minor bookkeeping issue — it's a 6x inflation.

**The 1.04 itself is on a specific implementation** (NoDOGE = BTC/ETH/SOL/XRP/DOGE). Base5 (with ADA) is 1.34. Different numbers depending on which assets you include.

**The progress chart 2.5 is milestone-aggregated** and explicitly NOT comparable to daily equity.

**Bottom line:** We claim "Sharpe 5.0+" in conversations but the honest daily equity number is 1.0-1.3. Report what you mean. Use the equity number on equity charts.

### 7. EXECUTION ASSUMPTIONS: BEST-CASE FAVORABLE

- Entry limit order at bar close → assumed filled at close price. In live trading, if signal fires at 00:01 UTC on a 1d bar that closed at 00:00, you get the NEXT day's open. That's one bar of slippage on every entry.
- Chandelier stop is a **market stop** — in a fast crash, fills can be 2-5% below the stop price. The 62.6% max drawdown in the backtest assumes instantaneous execution at the stop price.
- SOL position cap at $50K is a band-aid, not a solution. If the strategy signals 5 SOL positions simultaneously, that's $250K notional — 5× the per-asset cap.

### 8. BIGGEST BLIND SPOTS

1. **Only tested on crypto daily data.** Cross-market audit (SPY/QQQ/GLD) showed 3/8 pass. The edge on crypto is 3-5× larger than on equities. We are measuring edge in an environment that amplifies it (crypto's high volatility + strong trending = dream conditions for Turtle). If the strategy works on BTC at 0.87 Sharpe (SPY equivalent), that's genuinely useful. But the 1.04 on crypto is the SAME strategy with a 20% higher volatility regime — the excess Sharpe might just be vol arbitrage, not skill.

2. **Short selling is structurally absent.** We explicitly note "short side is a desert in crypto." In 2022 (BTC -65%), Turtle was +10.9%. In a true bear market where trend-following breaks down (flat or rising vol, no clean trends), the strategy relies on USDT hedge overlay. But that only reduces position to 30% — it doesn't profit from the decline.

3. **We only test on daily closing data.** All validation assumes you can enter at today's close when the signal fires. In live trading, the signal fires at 00:01 UTC and you either place a limit order for tomorrow's open or try to get filled today. The results could differ significantly.

4. **Transaction costs are under-reported.** We use 20bp RT (10bp/side). Realistically: maker 0% + taker 0.05% = 5bp RT for limit entries, but market exits on Chandelier stops are always taker. In a crash, spreads widen from 1-2bp to 10-20bp. The backtest's 20bp RT is optimistic for crash scenarios.
